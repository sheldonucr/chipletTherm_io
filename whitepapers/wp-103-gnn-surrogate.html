ThermStack-GNN: a package-trained surrogate for design-space exploration

AbstractWhen the same package must be evaluated hundreds or thousands of times, a trained model changes the economics of exploration. ThermStack-GNN predicts temperature fields for variations in power allocation and cooling within a learned operating range. In the demonstrated benchmark — a stepped 7 nm HBM+CPU package and 360 unseen power-and-cooling combinations — it returns a full 3D map in 10.6 ms at 0.32 K mean field RMSE against the FEM reference, a 207× query-time speedup. This paper reports the benchmark and describes how the surrogate fits a physics-verified workflow.

1 / THE BENCHMARK

360 unseen scenarios on one package

Use ThermStack-GNN to screen candidate scenarios, identify sensitive conditions and prioritize the cases that deserve a numerical check.

2 / USING THE SURROGATE

Fast exploration, physics in the loop

Explore alternatives quickly

Screen power budgets and cooling assumptions repeatedly, then inspect the thermal consequences on the package rather than relying on a single scalar limit.

Keep physics in the loop

Confirm shortlisted scenarios with numerical analysis. A new geometry or operating condition outside the trained range requires qualification before relying on its predictions.

3 / SCOPE

What the benchmark does and does not establish

Benchmark scope: a single package geometry and 360 unseen power-and-cooling combinations. Reported query timing excludes training and data generation; performance is hardware- and workload-dependent. These are not AMD-case timings or accuracy guarantees for arbitrary packages. The numerical and FEM analysis paths used for confirmation are described in WP-101.
