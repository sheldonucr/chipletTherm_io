ThermStack white paperWP-104November 2026

Resolution on demand: micrometre-scale hot spots on a full accelerator package

AbstractGPU power maps now carry hot spots of 20–60 µm, on packages 50–60 mm across. ThermStack APR (Adaptive Physical Reasoning) resolves them on demand. On an AMD MI350-inspired package with synthetic 10 µm GPU power maps, APR resolves all eight compute dies at 20 µm in 56 minutes and 106 GB of memory, on one CPU server.

1 / RESOLUTION ON DEMAND

20 µm on every compute die, on one server

The whole package stays in the model; fine resolution goes where the hot spots are.

The modeled package is 54 × 62 mm with eight compute dies (XCDs) at 950 W. A uniform 20 µm grid of the whole package would need an estimated 15 TB of memory. ThermStack APR reaches 20 µm on all eight XCDs within the memory of a single CPU server.

20 µmResolution on all eight compute dies of a 54 × 62 mm package
56 minWall time on one CPU server, no GPU
106 GBPeak memory; a uniform 20 µm grid is estimated at about 15 TB

2 / THE WORKLOAD

Synthetic 10 µm GPU power maps

Each XCD carries its own floorplan-style power map at 10 µm (1,100 × 1,100 pixels): compute-unit arrays, an I/O and cache spine, and 635–832 local hot spots of 20–60 µm, at up to 5,353 W/cm². Package geometry, materials and cooling are those of the WP-102 model.

MI350-inspired model with synthetic power maps, not AMD data. Steady state. Wall time and memory from one 2026 CPU server; they will differ on other hardware.
