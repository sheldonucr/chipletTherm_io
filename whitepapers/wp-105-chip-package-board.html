ThermStack white paperWP-105November 2026

Chip, package and board in one model: an MI350-class OAM module

AbstractA GPU module is designed in three tools: the chip stack in 3Dblox, the package and the board in PCB layout tools. ThermStack reads all three design files and builds one thermal model: an MI350-class OAM module at 974 W on a 102 × 165 mm board. ThermStack APR resolves the hottest compute die at 50 µm in 1 h 20 min and 110 GB of memory, on one CPU server.

1 / FROM DESIGN FILES TO ONE MODEL

Three design files, one thermal model

Each level of the module comes in the format its own design team already uses.

LevelInput fileWhat it brings
BoardIPC-2581Stack-up, copper on every layer, vias, part placement, VRM stages and their power
PackageIPC-2581Build-up and core stack-up, copper, microvias and core vias, C4 bumps, BGA balls, capacitors
Chip stack3Dblox 2.1 + CSV power mapsInterposer, compute and I/O dies, HBM stacks, bonding layers, 10 µm power map on each compute die

The board file places the package at its GPU site; the package places the chip stack on its top side. ThermStack aligns the three levels, resolves the copper of the board and package layouts, and joins them into a single model with one set of boundary conditions. No hand-built geometry and no manual stack-up entry.

2 / THE DESIGN

An MI350-class OAM module

The module follows the public outline of an OCP Accelerator Module (OAM) with an MI350-class GPU. All three layouts are synthetic, written for this study.

LevelSizeConstructionPower
Board102 × 165 mm, ~2.0 mm thick12 copper layers; 16 VRM stages with inductors and bulk capacitors; dog-bone fan-out and thermal vias under the VRM stages24 W (16 × 1.5 W)
Package64 × 72 mm, ~0.96 mm thick5-2-5 ABF build-up, 12 copper layers; 83,700 C4 bumps; 4,608 BGA balls; capacitors on the top side—
Chip stack54 × 62 mm interposer8 compute dies (XCD), 2 I/O dies (IOD), 8 HBM3E 12-high stacks, carrier silicon950 W
CoolingCold plate on the module footprint (10,000 W/m²K); 10 W/m²K on the rest of the board top and 50 W/m²K under the board; ambient 25 °C
974 WModule power: 950 W chip stack, 24 W voltage regulators
88,000+Solder joints: 83,700 C4 bumps and 4,608 BGA balls
137,000Vias in the package and board layouts, each one from the design file

3 / RESULTS

50 µm on the hottest compute die, with the whole module in the model

The board, the package and all eight compute dies stay in one model; resolution goes where the hot spots are.

50 µmResolution on the hottest compute die, XCD_6: peak 394.5 K (121.3 °C)
1 h 20 minWall time on one CPU server, no GPU
110 GBPeak memory; a uniform 50 µm grid of the module is estimated at about 3.7 TB

Where the heat goes

The cold plate removes 98.5 % of the 974 W; 1.5 % (14.5 W) leaves through the board. Small as it is, the board still matters. The VRM stages reach about 382 K at 1.5 W each, on board cooling alone, within 13 K of the compute dies; only a model that includes the board shows them. And the module runs cooler than the chip stack alone: modelled on its own, with a plain substrate and the cold plate on the stack, at the same resolution, XCD_6 reads 6.8 K hotter, because the package spreads heat under the cold plate.

MI350-class module with synthetic layouts and power maps, not AMD data. Steady state. Wall time and memory from one 2026 CPU server; they will differ on other hardware.
